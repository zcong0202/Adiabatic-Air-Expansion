AA OUTDOOR COOLING SEAT: MATHEMATICAL VIABILITY
19 October 2026. Four LZB46-RL-A025-11 motors in parallel.
Status: conditional feasibility. No seat CFD or hardware validation.

1. EVIDENCE AND SCOPE
The supplied DOCX defines a proposed environment, not measured boundary conditions.
It assumes 0.078 kg/s at 1 C, 30/35 C ambient, 55% RH, 0/0.5/1 m/s wind,
a seated person and about 1 m3 occupied region. It provides no seat geometry.
The listed A-D outlets are controls. Custom nozzles remain candidates.
The previous 20 cm / 5x5 cm / >6 K target is a separate historical local target.
Do not treat previous vortex CFD results as seat predictions.

Manufacturer evidence, retrieved 19 October:
- Exact motor: 0.84 kW maximum shaft output, 1270 rpm, 6.3 Nm,
  16.2 atmospheric L/s air use at maximum output, 10 mm inlet hose,
  16 mm exhaust hose. Lubrication-free vane motor, not a turbine.
- Manufacturer guide: catalogue data apply at 6.3 bar gauge. Air flow is
  atmospheric expanded volume, not compressed volume in the motor.
- Product page says 7 bar maximum; exact ATEX manual says 6.3 bar/nameplate.
  Use 6.3 bar until the supplier resolves the difference.
- Exact manual: 40 C maximum surrounding AND compressed-air inlet temperature.
- Air consumption varies with speed/load and is greatest at free speed.
  Four full-power motors therefore require the correct external load.

2. MASS AND SHAFT WORK
FAD_total = 4 * 16.2 * 60 / 1000 = 3.888 m3/min.
This is a common-reference free-air comparison. Never multiply by supply bar.
The nominal mass conversion uses the DOCX value 19.4 g/s per motor:
mdot = 0.0776 kg/s; cp = 1006 J/(kg K); C = mdot cp = 78.0656 W/K.
The exact catalogue volume reference temperature/humidity must be harmonised
with the compressor's FAD reference. The page does not state that temperature.
For example dry air at 20 C and 101325 Pa gives about 0.0780 kg/s total.
These small reference differences are below the source thermal uncertainty.
Pshaft = 4 * 840 = 3360 W.
Pshaft_each = torque * 2 pi * rpm / 60; catalogue rounding closes within 1%.

Steady open-system first law, heat into motor positive:
mdot * (h01 - h02) = Pshaft - Qmotor.
h0 = h + V^2/2; ignore elevation. Evaluate bulk states after velocity recovery.
In a constant-cp approximation:
Tmotor = Tin - (Pshaft - Qmotor)/C.
Tsupply = Tin + duct_gain - (Pshaft - Qmotor)/C.
Qcold = C * (Ta - Tsupply)
      = Pshaft - C*(Tin-Ta) - Qmotor - C*duct_gain.

At Ta=30 C, Tin=40 C, Qmotor=0, prescribed duct_gain=4 K:
Tmotor = -3.0407 C; Tsupply = 0.9593 C; Qcold = 2267.1 W.
The DOCX's -3 C -> 1 C -> about 2.3 kW is internally consistent.
It is NOT an exhaust temperature guarantee.
The 4 K gain uses 312.3 W. A 1 C outlet permits only 315.4 W total
motor-plus-duct heat gain at the catalogue shaft work and chosen mass flow.
Thus the nominal 1 C value has almost no additional heat-gain margin.
An 8 C outlet permits 861.9 W total heat gain.
Every additional 100 W into the collected cold air raises its temperature
by 1.281 K at the reference flow. Every 1 K of inlet rise costs 78.1 W.

The constant +4 K path is a prescribed heat-budget sensitivity, not a universal
passive-duct law. For real duct sizing use:
Ts = Ta + (Tmotor-Ta)*exp(-UA/C).
Nominal +4 K here corresponds to UA about 10.1 W/K at Ta=30 C.
This path heats a subambient stream and cools a superambient one.
No fixed heat-transfer coefficient or 0.5-second warm-up time is universal.

3. EXPANSION METHOD AND LOAD
Ideal isentropic reference, absolute pressures and kelvin:
T2s = T1*(p2/p1)^((gamma-1)/gamma), gamma=1.4.
eta_shaft = Pshaft / [mdot*cp*(T1-T2s)].
At 6.3 bar gauge -> 1.01325 bar absolute, Tin=313.15 K:
eta_shaft is about 0.319. This explains the document's assumed 32%.
It is inferred from the catalogue power/flow pair, NOT an independent measured
refrigeration efficiency. Motor/body heat exchange remains unmeasured.
CoolProp HEOS::Air cross-check, same shaft-specific work and Qmotor=0:
eta_shaft about 0.320; recovered exhaust about -4.32 C;
with prescribed +4 K path, supply about -0.32 C.
A real-gas regulator alone gives about 38.72 C from 40 C here.
The ideal-gas throttle preserves enthalpy and recovered bulk temperature.
Therefore low-pressure throttling is not a replacement for loaded expansion.
No 200-bar chain is needed for this specific motor concept.

At fixed rated mass and prescribed +4 K duct rise, 75% shaft work gives
11.72 C supply; 50% gives 22.48 C. These are energy sensitivities only.
Actual flow changes with motor load. Do not use them as a motor curve.
Free running gives zero external shaft power, not the rated cooling mechanism.
Choose a commercial generator/load or brake with speed control to absorb
roughly 0.84 kW at 1270 rpm per motor. No matching product is selected here.
Put its dissipated heat outside the occupied region and cold path.
If the entire 3.36 kW returns as heat to the cooled zone, it cancels the
shaft-work cooling term. With the reference heat assumptions, net heat is
added, even before ambient exchange. A fan powered inside this air stream
also eventually returns its shaft work as heat to that stream/space.

4. RENTAL COMPRESSOR AND AIR TREATMENT
Live manufacturer table: XAS 38/48/58/68/78/88/98 provide respectively
2/2.5/3/3.6/4.5/5/5.4 m3/min at 7 bar gauge.
The linked brochure gives XAS68 as 3.5 rather than 3.6, and XAHS38 as 2.3
rather than 2.0. Both versions fail four full-power motors; resolve actual unit.
XAS78 (4.5 at 7 bar) is the first listed candidate meeting the bare 3.888
budget and an ASSUMED 15% flow allowance (4.471 m3/min).
Its bare spare capacity is only 15.7%. A dryer purge, leak or hot-weather
capacity loss can consume this. XAS88 (5 at 7 bar) has 28.6% bare margin.
Neither is selected/approved without pressure drops and actual rental options.
7 bar supply leaves about 0.7 bar above the 6.3 bar motor operating point
for dryer, filters, regulator, supply hose and distribution, before derating.
The higher-pressure XATS68/XAHS38 do not solve the four-motor flow deficit.

The compressor's aftercooler and separator are options. They do not establish
a refrigerated dryer, +3 C pressure dew point, or a guaranteed Tin <=40 C.
At 35 C ambient, the DOCX +10 K aftercooler assumption produces 45 C inlet,
which is outside this motor manual's limit. Require inlet approach <=5 K
at that ambient, or a separately cooled final supply. Derating has no vendor
map here and cannot silently be assumed to retain the same output.

5. EXHAUST NETWORK AND NOZZLES
At 1 C/1 atm, actual supply volume is about 0.0603 m3/s.
V=Q/A, qdynamic=rho*V^2/2, Ar=g*(Ta-Ts)/Ta_kelvin*Dh/V^2.
Controls: A holes 0.603 m/s (face average 0.121); B 150 mm 3.41 m/s;
C four 70 mm 3.92 m/s; D plate holes 0.482 and two 50 mm outlets 3.07.
The document's bulk exit numbers are consistent.
Hole size, pattern, plate resistance and branch balancing are still unspecified.
Face average speed alone cannot establish local buoyancy-dominated hole jets.

A motor's 16 mm recommended exhaust hose carries about 15 L/s:
V about 75 m/s; kinetic head about 3.6 kPa. A 1 m straight length gives
about 4.5 kPa Darcy loss with an ASSUMED f=0.02.
This is a screening estimate; use a compressible network for detailed sizing.
Kinetic head is not all irreversible loss; an efficient diffuser can recover it.
At 40 mm branch ID: about 12 m/s and 46 Pa/m at the same reference flow.
Expand promptly into larger branches, then collect into the 150 mm duct.
Do not infer negligible motor back pressure from the final outlet's 10 Pa.
The DOCX 1-2 kPa flag is a screening threshold, not a vendor-validated cutoff.

Reuse nozzle concepts, not their old dimensions or CFD predictions.
A historical 28 mm outlet at this per-motor flow gives about 24.5 m/s.
The old 8 mm throat gives an incompressible velocity about 300 m/s,
which flags a compressible/back-pressure constraint, not a valid design state.
A custom slot with total open area 0.015 m2 gives about 4 m/s; e.g.
2 x (150 mm x 50 mm) outlets. This is a CAD seed, not an optimal nozzle.
Include low-loss diffusers, directional slots and hybrid outlets in new tests.
For an adiabatic passive nozzle: h0 conserved. It can change mixing, reach,
capture and static temperature, but cannot create extra stagnation cooling.

6. OCCUPIED-ZONE BALANCE
Let k = fraction of source flow actually entering a chosen uniform zone,
Cz=k*mdot*cp; mw=ambient exchange mass flow, W=mw*cp.
L = net heat to zone from occupant, solar-heated panels and other sources,
    plus any returned shaft-work heat. Do not count one heat source twice.
Steady balance:
(Cz+W)*Tz = Cz*Ts + W*Ta + L.
Delta = Ta-Tz = [Cz*(Ta-Ts)-L]/(Cz+W).
For target Delta >= d >0:
W <= [Cz*(Ta-Ts-d)-L]/d.
Equivalent warm mass limit:
mw <= [k*mdot*cp*(Ttarget-Ts)-L]/[cp*(Ta-Ttarget)].
This is a conditional UNIFORM model, not a pointwise torso/neck prediction.

At Ta30, Ts0.9593, k1, target20, L100 W:
max warm exchange = 0.13782 kg/s = 0.11836 ambient m3/s.
A 100 W person is only a small part of this system's air-cooling capacity.
Ambient exchange, source spill and heated panels can dominate the budget.
At k0.5: allowable warm exchange drops to 0.05491 m3/s.
At L600 W and k1: it drops to 0.07567 m3/s.
For only a 6 K zone drop (target24), use the separate threshold in JSON.

A crude wind exchange closure: mw = rho_ambient * Cd * A * Vwind.
Only the PRODUCT Cd*A is meaningful in this screen.
With Cd1, 0.5 m/s wind requires effective A <=0.2367 m2;
at 1 m/s, effective A <=0.1184 m2, before added sun/panel heat or spill.
One effective m2 at those wind speeds gives about 26.7 / 28.3 C uniform
zone temperature in the dry baseline. These are illustrative exchange-model
results, not a prescribed seat opening size. Real exchange needs CFD.
Shelter direction, buoyancy, wind recirculation and outlet placement set Cd/A/k.

Transient uniform zone, fixed gas mass M, with matched outlet mass flow:
M*cp*dTz/dt = Cz*(Ts-Tz) + W*(Ta-Tz) + L.
Tz(t)=Tsteady+(Tinitial-Tsteady)*exp(-t/tau),
tau=M*cp/(Cz+W).
Continuous supply renews the cold air. No universal 0.5 s reheating rule follows.
A buffer changes peak flow and duty; it cannot increase average cold production.
A pressurised warm receiver can smooth compressor supply. A cold buffer is
not needed to establish the present continuous-flow concept.

7. MOISTURE, CFD AND SUCCESS CONDITIONS
At +3 C pressure dew point and 7.31325 bar absolute, humid-air calculation
gives source water ratio about 0.000662 kg/kg dry air. This supports the DOCX
0.00065 estimate. At 30 C/55% RH ambient and 1 C source, all 1001 sampled
enthalpy-conserving mixing ratios remain unsaturated; peak RH about 69.3%.
Across 30/35 C, supply -3/1/8 C, maximum about 91.8%.
This supports a no-fog nominal external mixing screen only. It does not
exclude internal transient icing, liquid carryover, or condensation on cold
surfaces. Wet motor air can change both reliability and thermal performance.

First correct the source/network, THEN run the seat CFD.
Use 3D variable-density buoyant flow. Around 30 K contrast at 303 K,
density changes about 10%; Boussinesq needs an accuracy check.
The prior constant-density axisymmetric jet solver cannot predict this seat.
Model person with EITHER a 100 W heat input split between convection/radiation,
OR fixed skin temperature and predicted heat transfer. Both cannot be imposed
as independent constraints on the same boundary.
Include radiation/solar/panel heat, pressure losses and wind two directions.
Use paired source states from a work/flow/heat budget, not free temperature knobs.
Include scalar transport to distinguish source capture from cooling utilisation.
Retain A-D controls; add custom slot/diffuser/hybrid candidates.
Measure occupied air temperatures separately from cold-zone volume and human
heat removal. Define torso/neck averaging regions and temporal averaging.
Do not accept a lower cold pool as proof of upper-body cooling.

Minimum computational gates:
- Source: coupled catalogue pair, external work sink, motor <=40 C and6.3 bar.
- Network: pressure-specific FAD, drying/heat budget and exhaust back pressure.
- Seat: still-air buoyancy, then 0.5/1 m/s front and side wind.
- Numerics: mesh/time-step sensitivity, mass/enthalpy/scalar closure and stable
  occupied-region statistics; compare turbulence/scalar diffusivity choices.
- Claim: target20 C torso/neck and useful person heat removal remain UNPROVED
  until these cases resolve the capture and warm-exchange rates.

8. FORMAL/Numerical STATUS
Six new Lean theorems check source target inequality, zone deficit identity,
zone target inequality, shaft-heat cancellation, four-motor FAD threshold,
and one concrete reference certificate.
The reference certificate assumes fully captured source, heat pickup <=312.2624W,
ambient30, inlet40, shaft3360W, warm thermal flow <=120.72W/K (=0.12 kg/s),
100W net load, and the steady source/zone balances. It proves Tzone <20 C.
The equations, catalogue pair, capture, heat and ambient-exchange bounds are
hypotheses. Lean does not prove that the real seat satisfies them.
CoolProp EOS and psychrometrics are numerical tools, not kernel-verified physics.
Run assess.py for calculations; see verification.json and Lean build.log.

9. EXECUTED CFD/CAD/LEAN LOOP (second phase)
The user accepted the reference enclosure,0.9 m clear width,0.85 m depth,
1.8 m height; open front, side panels, roof. All actual hardware is still absent.
Read results/cfd-cad-loop.json for the latest executed results and checks.
Read output/pdf/AA-seat-CFD-CAD-Lean-iteration.pdf for the iteration report.

The reference source0.0776 kg/s is four motors. Three active motors give
0.0582 kg/s and2.52 kW shaft extraction. Per-motor power/flow stays paired.
The same UA10.074 W/K warms that lower mass more; source is about2.18 C.
This is more consistent than keeping a fixed4 K rise as mass is reduced.
The normal three-active mode is a candidate within a four-installed system.
Do not interpret it as a supplier-approved motor/load assembly or duty scheme.

CFD solves a variable-density perfect-gas energy equation with gravity,
not the old constant-density axisymmetric jet model. It transports a source
origin scalar using mass flux. Heat from a100 W block manikin enters the air.
Solar/radiation, water species and actual motor internals are not resolved.

Two sample air regions have fixed, explicit bounds in every case:
Torso: x0.42..0.62, y-0.22..0.22, z0.72..1.16 m.
Neck: x0.42..0.62, y-0.16..0.16, z1.20..1.40 m.
The mean is a VOLUME mean of air, not skin temperature, not mass-flux mean,
and not the earlier5x5 cm area target. Weight each cell by its EXACT Cartesian
intersection volume with that box. Use piecewise-constant cell fields and
trapezoid time averaging over the last4 saved seconds. A mean <=20 C does
not imply every point in that region is <=20 C. Fraction below20 is also reported.

The design loop tests rear delivery against four side slots, then reduces
active source count and tests a finer mesh. Internal CAD-linked RANS rejects
a50mm spigot feeding a140x80mm slot because of severe reverse flow. A100mm
spigot reduces expansion and exit nonuniformity. Its short URANS follow-up
supplies an actual mean velocity/turbulence profile back to the external CFD.
The CAD and CFD share a205mm flow-body length and140x80mm exit area.
The final100mm hose spigot has95.2mm clear inlet and2.4mm nominal wall.
All custom parts are low-pressure exhaust nozzles. Commercial supply parts
and seat/source envelopes are not complete manufacturing/installation CAD.

For interpreting geometry-to-cooling, define alpha as the volume/time mean
cold-origin mass fraction in the SAME sampling region. Define an excess term
E = Tmean - [Ta-(Ta-Ts)*alpha]. This is a diagnostic from the CFD fields.
Then, by construction:
Delta = alpha*(Ta-Ts)-E,
alpha_required_for_target = (Ta-Ttarget+E)/(Ta-Ts), if Ta>Ts.
Do not treat E and alpha as fixed across a new source, wind or geometry.
The diagnostics separate source contrast, delivered cold fraction and residual
warming, but they do not independently validate the flow or thermal model.

An admissible combined upward error e must obey:
Tcomputed+e <= Ttarget.
For the older strict6K objective:
Tcomputed+e < Ta-6.
The margin from the CFD mean to each threshold is reported. A grid difference
is NOT a proved bound on e. Lean checks this conditional error-budget logic.
It does not prove the continuum flow, turbulence, supplier map or hardware.

The source must retain its stated UA/heat budget after the CAD change.
Use commercial insulated cold-air branches and budget collector, branch and
nozzle heat pickup together. Increasing a branch diameter reduces speed/loss,
but can increase exposed area. Nozzle CAD does not establish its thermal UA.

The atmospheric pressure boundary was refined after a no-flow check revealed
small spurious motion. Final cases use the exact isothermal hydrostatic
ambient-pressure profile and matching initial pressure. Preliminary cases remain
in the record with their original boundary assumptions. Initial failed meshes,
rejected pressure setups and interrupted cases are not accepted designs.
