Archimedes number: Ar = g·(ΔT/T)·D/V². When Ar ≪ 0.1 the jet is momentum-dominated: it travels forward and only sinks further downstream. When Ar ≫ 1 it is buoyancy-dominated and the cold air drops straight down.
4. Key outputs to report
Temperature around the occupant: can torso and neck height reach ≤ 20 °C (ΔT ≥ 10 K)?
Cold-zone volume: volume below 22 °C, and how it changes as wind goes from 0 to 1 m/s.
Air speed at the body: above about 0.8–1 m/s at the neck or face feels like an uncomfortable draught; too low and it isn't noticed.
Jet decay (options B, C, D): distance at which the 1 °C jet warms to 20 °C.
Cooling utilisation: fraction of the supplied cooling that stays within about 1 m³ around the occupant.
5. Modelling notes
Start the domain at the supply outlet. No need to model the motors or the plenum; use the inlet conditions above.
No inlet pressure needs to be set. The motors exhaust close to atmospheric pressure. Duct and nozzle losses are typically tens of Pa, and outlet dynamic pressure is only about 10 Pa at 4 m/s, so motor operation is essentially unaffected.
Flag any high-loss design. If an outlet option's total pressure drop exceeds about 1–2 kPa, back pressure on the motors will reduce cooling, and the inlet conditions will need revising.
Assumptions: motor isentropic efficiency about 32 %, aftercooler outlet 10 K above ambient, about 4 K heat gain in the duct. Once we have measured motor data, the supply temperature is the first value to update.
