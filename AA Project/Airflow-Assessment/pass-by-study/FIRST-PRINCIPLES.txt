CONFIRMED
Open outdoor installation. People walk past outlets. No seat or enclosure.
Compare nearest nominal source-to-person distances 0.2, 0.35, 0.5 m.
Valve open for at most 30 s. This is not each person's exposure duration.
Use stock source/control hardware; custom nozzle geometry is allowed.

SOURCE BUDGET (CONDITIONAL)
Steady control volume, neglect source kinetic and potential energy changes:
  mdot * (h_in - h_out) = P_shaft - Q_into_air
With constant cp:
  T_motor = T_in - (P_shaft - Q_into_air)/(mdot*cp)
Cold-path heat leak to ambient, constant UA:
  T_supply = T_ambient + (T_motor-T_ambient)*exp(-UA/(mdot*cp))
Available sensible cold-air power:
  Q_cold = mdot*cp*(T_ambient-T_supply)

Nominal hypothesis inherited from the loaded-motor source calculation:
4 motors; 0.0194 kg/s and 840 W shaft work each; inlet 40 C;
zero nominal motor heat pickup; UA about 10.074 W/K.
At ambient 30 C: mdot=0.0776 kg/s, supply about 0.959 C,
Q_cold about 2267 W. Demand=3.888 m3/min free-air equivalent.
Motor load, exact flow reference state, heat pickup, treatment, collectors,
backpressure and commercial interfaces remain unverified.
Shaft-work dissipation must stay outside the cooled stream and target.

METHOD CHOICE
An adiabatic regulator/valve with no shaft work approximates throttling:
  h_out = h_in  (isenthalpic)
For an ideal gas this does not lower the temperature. Real-air
Joule-Thomson cooling needs a real-gas state calculation; it is not the
same process as work extraction.
For reversible adiabatic ideal-gas expansion:
  T_out_s/T_in = (P_out/P_in)^((gamma-1)/gamma)  (isentropic)
Use kelvin and absolute pressures. An actual loaded motor is irreversible:
  eta_is = (h_in-h_out)/(h_in-h_out_s)
The rated flow/work pair must determine the actual enthalpy drop.
Do not choose an independent low source temperature and full rated flow.
At current nominal mass/cp, 3360 W gives only about 43.04 K of dry
temperature drop before heat pickup. Inlet 40 C therefore gives about
-3.04 C motor exhaust, not -40 or -100 C. The nominal path then warms it.
Additional motors of the same type in parallel scale power and mass
together; they mainly increase total cooling capacity, not this specific
enthalpy drop. A different source method needs a new paired state map.

A BUFFER changes timing, not conservation of energy or free-jet mixing.
Useful stored cold energy cannot exceed integrated source cold power
minus storage and delivery heat gain. Higher pulse mass flow needs a
matched flow path and source/storage model. A cold reservoir cannot stop
ambient entrainment after discharge. This study assumes no cold buffer.

For an ideal 30 s event at full nominal source state:
  m_air = mdot*t = 2.328 kg
  V_free_air = 3.888*30/60 = 1.944 m3
  E_cold = integral Q_cold dt = about 68.0 kJ
This energy is supplied to the outdoor air. It is not energy removed from
one human. Cold-line startup and motor/valve transients are not established.

WHY THERE IS NO UNIVERSAL 0.5 SECOND REHEATING TIME
In the low-speed external flow, temperature is transported by advection
and molecular/turbulent diffusion. Entrainment mixes ambient mass into the
cold stream. Geometry, velocity, wind and density change that mixing.
A parcel-specific exponential needs a known heat-transfer time constant.
It cannot be imposed as a universal free-jet lifetime.

Ideal dry adiabatic two-stream mixing, equal cp:
  T_mix = f_cold*T_supply + (1-f_cold)*T_ambient
  Delta = f_cold*(T_ambient-T_supply)
For Delta>6 K at nominal source/ambient:
  f_cold > 6/(30-0.959...) = 0.206606...
  warm_mass/cold_mass < (30-0.959...)/6 - 1 = 3.84012...
These are enthalpy conditions, not a footprint or a personal benefit claim.
For nonzero external heat, moist air or nonuniform cp, use the full
enthalpy balance. Do not equate a mass fraction with a geometric area.

MOTION AND VALVE TIMING
Assume a straight path at v=1.2 m/s; sweep 0.6,1.2,1.8 m/s.
For established cooled width W along the walking path:
  t_crossing = W/v
Hypothetical W=0.3 m gives 0.25 s, not 30 s.
Neither the nozzle slot width nor nozzle-to-person distance is W.
W must be measured from the resolved temperature field at the target.
Actual exposure is the intersection of passage, valve/jet arrival and
the region where the moving patch meets the required temperature.

Screening scales, not trajectory predictions:
  U_outlet = mdot/(rho_supply*sum(outlet_area))
  t_jet ~ standoff/representative_jet_speed
  trigger_lead_distance ~ walking_speed*(valve_delay+line_delay+t_jet)
Real jets slow, spread and bend; a cold line may need substantial startup.
Source state can change during valve switching. These upstream dynamics
are absent from the first external CFD screen.

EVALUATION OF CFD
Solve buoyant dry ideal-gas URANS with energy and a source-origin scalar.
Use ground and short nozzle blocks. No walls, seat, roof or human obstacle.
Nominal cold flow starts at the outlet at t=0. Screening runs last10s;
selected controls last30s. Valve shutdown is not simulated.
It is a prescribed ideal outlet event, not a simulated regulator/motor.

For each virtual moving 5 x 5 cm air square A(t):
  T_area(t) = integral_A T(x,t) dA / area(A)
  Delta_area(t) = T_ambient - T_area(t)
Report peak deficit, total and longest continuous time above 6 K,
integral max(Delta_area,0) dt in K s, and area-mean speed.
Sample passages with centre crossings at 1.5,5,15,28.5 s after activation.
Square quadrature is 3x3 Gauss; native probes use cellPoint interpolation.
Moving traces interpolate fixed probe data in time and along the path.
K s is an air-exposure metric. It is not joules removed from skin.

For an ASSUMED upward temperature error bound e:
  T_real <= T_computed + e
  Delta_computed > 6+e implies Delta_real > 6
Report duration sensitivity at e=0,1,2,3 K. These e values are hypothetical,
not established physical error bounds. Grid comparisons alone do not prove e.
