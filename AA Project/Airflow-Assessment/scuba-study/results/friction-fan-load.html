AM0701: friction brake or fan

Motor received. Adjustable friction brake selected as first test candidate. Calculated loads below are design targets. No loaded motor point, brake fit or outdoor cooling result is verified.

Maximum radius: 20 cm, taken from the user’s available envelope. A 10 cm contact radius is used for examples. A heavy flywheel is unnecessary for steady loading.

Motor limits

Cosmostar AM0701: 0.9 HP (671 W), max 3 N·m, max 3000 RPM, operating range 1–7 bar, 2.61 kg. Published max consumption: 750 L/min at 6 bar / 3000 RPM. These are separate maxima. Confirm actual unit nameplate. Exact shaft drawing and loaded performance map remain unavailable.

15 g/s assumes the 750 L/min figure is free-air flow at 1.2 kg/m³. This basis is unverified. A 22°C cylinder does not fix motor inlet temperature after pressure reduction. Use regulated, flow-qualified supply; never connect the scuba cylinder pressure directly to the motor.

Brake geometry

τ = Fₜr = μNtotal r. Fₜ is total tangential friction force. Ntotal is the sum of pad normal forces. At fixed power and RPM, doubling radius halves both forces. It does not change torque, work or heat generation.

Use a purpose-made friction lining on a balanced, speed-rated disc or drum. Use a rigid frame, guarded coupling and external support where needed. Do not press an abrasive against an improvised flywheel. Keep pad dust and brake heat out of the exhaust. Verify motor shaft radial/axial limits before applying side load. Brake torque is measured from reaction force × reaction-arm length; this is a different radius from pad contact radius.

At 400 W, the brake receives 4 kJ per 10 s pulse; at 600 W, 6 kJ. A simplified 0.5 kg steel heat sink at c = 500 J/kg/K rises 16 K / 24 K if it receives all this heat uniformly. Pad contact temperatures can be much higher. This heat-capacity check does not qualify a brake. Repeated pulses need cooling and a duty rating.

Does a propeller fit the envelope?

Pfan = Cₚ ρ n³ D⁵, with n in revolutions/second and D as diameter. Cₚ is the aerodynamic power coefficient, distinct from air heat capacity cₚ.

Primary example: UIUC’s measured APC 14×10 E static data give Cₚ = 0.042579 at 2960 RPM. Diameter 35.56 cm fits the envelope. At assumed density 1.2 kg/m³, calculated absorption is 34.9 W, giving only 1.73 K motor drop at assumed 15 g/s / 25% heat return. This is aerodynamic data, not an AM0701 test.

Even a 40 cm propeller at 3000 RPM and assumed Cₚ = 0.04–0.12 absorbs only 61–184 W. These coefficient limits are sensitivities, not universal fan limits. To absorb 200 / 400 / 600 W at this diameter and RPM needs Cₚ = 0.130 / 0.260 / 0.391. A selected fan or blower needs its own power curve. The tested small propeller is a weak candidate for the 400 W target. Do not modify blades or exceed their speed rating. Fan thrust, bearing loads and warm slipstream also affect mounting and jet mixing. Direct the slipstream away from the cold plume.

Explore a conditional operating point

Changing power and flow independently is a sensitivity. The real combination must satisfy the measured motor/load curves. The calculator does not predict flow or an operating RPM.

First test

Start with light brake load. Record actual RPM, brake reaction torque, mass flow, motor inlet pressure/temperature and collected exhaust pressure/temperature together. Seek 200 W, then 400 W. Attempt 600 W only if stable within the actual motor and brake limits. Excess load stalls the motor: at zero speed, shaft power is zero. Do not infer useful power from torque alone.

During spin-up, a flywheel absorbs Iω dω/dt. At steady speed its stored energy stops increasing. For a 10 s cold pulse, warm motor metal can erase early cooling. The transient energy balance is ṁcₚ(Tin − Tout) = Pshaft − Q̇into + dEcv/dt, neglecting KE/PE changes. Measure the time trace. Compare a regulator-only bypass at matched actual mass flow. Then measure the 5×5 cm mean temperature at 20 and 50 cm.
