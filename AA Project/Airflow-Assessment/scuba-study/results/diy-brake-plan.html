Measure actual brake power

P = Fperpendicular × Larm × 2π × RPM / 60. Use a properly constrained torque-reaction fixture and calibrated continuous force measurement. The reaction-arm radius is different from rotor contact radius. Caliper cable tension alone does not measure shaft torque. A fixed bracket cannot be converted into a torque sensor by putting a scale on its adjuster.

Before ordering a coupling or printing fitted parts

The user’s approximate “4 cm” dimension does not establish shaft diameter or usable length. The workshop must measure shaft diameter, usable protrusion, key/flat/thread and retention, motor mounting pattern, and the chosen hub. Do not order a 40 mm-bore coupling from this estimate.

Have the workshop define the rotor/hub speed and balance limits, the metal mounting/reaction fixture and guard. Then design printed accessories around those measured interfaces. Fit temperature monitoring and an accessible air shutoff. Measure RPM, torque, airflow and motor temperatures together. No generator or separate flywheel is required.
