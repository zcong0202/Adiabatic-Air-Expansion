How to show cooling legitimately
  1. Bench-only first: standard Gast exhaust is lubricated. Collect/vent it away from people; do not use it as the person-facing cooling outlet.
  2. Use the existing rated cylinder pressure-reduction package. Set motor running pressure to its chosen curve point, not cylinder pressure.
  3. Use a guarded, rated brake or generator/load with torque and rpm measurement. For the lower-duty 2AM target: about 200 W and 1.91 Nm at 1000 rpm. This is a target, not a purchased complete load station.
  4. Record tank pressure/temperature, motor inlet pressure/temperature, exhaust pressure, low-speed bulk exhaust temperature, actual mass flow and shaft torque/rpm simultaneously.
  5. Use a short insulated, low-mass collection section; its UA=1 W/K calculation is a design sensitivity, not measured conductance. Keep exhaust pressure loss low and measure it.
  6. Run a regulator-only bypass at comparable measured flow; it separates Joule-Thomson/line cooling from incremental motor cooling. Record any flow change rather than treating unlike cases as a controlled comparison.
  7. Precondition until the measured source state is repeatable, with exhaust away from the target. Then record repeated up-to-10 s demonstration pulses. Do not promise first-pulse cooling: warm motor/nozzle metal can absorb much of the nominal cooling budget.
  8. Show cooling with calibrated simultaneous temperatures, not a cold housing, IR image or skin sensation. Use a predeclared demonstration screen such as a repeatable >=5 K bulk drop across the motor with sensor uncertainty much smaller than that drop; this is a test criterion, not a predicted result.
  9. Check steady first-law closure: Q_into_motor = Wshaft - mdot cp (Tin-Tout), with kinetic-temperature correction if required. During startup include motor/fixture thermal storage; do not interpret it as steady heat leak.
  10. A regulator-only measured cold-air result is a legitimate throttling POC. It does not validate a motor expander or prove outdoor 20-50 cm cooling.

The standard Gast models above need lubrication. LL/NLP models have different pressure limits (manual: 5.5 bar), so they require separate curves and calculations. New cold-air temperatures are first-law scenarios; no new spatial CFD or 20–50 cm result is claimed.

Equations / curves / uncertainty
ṁ = ρfree Qfree
ΔTmotor = (Wshaft − Qinto_motor)/(ṁ cp)
Tnozzle = Ta + (Tin − ΔTmotor − Ta) exp[−UA/(ṁ cp)]
t = V(ρfill−ρcutoff)/ṁ

Heat into the motor is zero in the nominal calculation. JSON includes a 25% shaft-heat-return scenario and ±10% power/flow sensitivity. These are assumptions, not error bounds. At startup, motor/fixture thermal storage must be included.
