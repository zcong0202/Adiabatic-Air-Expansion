Dry-air first principles · examples are calculations, not new CFD

Mass flow supplies cooling power. Pressure supports expansion and flow. The nozzle controls where that cooling reaches and how much warm air mixes in.

1 · Mass flow supplies cooling power

Q̇ = ṁ cp (Ta − Tc)
Minjected = ṁ t

More g/s means more cooling energy each second. Grams are stored mass; g/s is the delivery rate.

At 0.96°C supply: 2 g/s ≈58 W; 77 g/s ≈2.25 kW. A 10 s spray injects 20 g or 770 g.

Assumptions / derivation

Reported vortex case: 2 g/s at 5°C against 30°C has only about 50 W sensible capacity. This explains a limited budget, not the exact cause of the reported test failure. Not all source air reaches the target. Ideal cooling capacity is not skin heat removal.

2 · The nozzle sets delivery

U0 = ṁ / (ρ n Ae)
J ≈ ṁ U0

Area, shape, count and aim change reach, spread and mixing. Small passages add backpressure; oversized outlets can lose useful momentum.

77 g/s through four 280 × 40 mm exits → 1.33 m/s. The nozzle redistributes the cold source; it does not add refrigeration.

Assumptions / derivation

Uniform equal exits and near-atmospheric pressure are assumed. J is exit momentum flux; pressure thrust must be added for an overpressured jet. At this speed, kinetic recovery changes static temperature by only 0.0009 K. Re = ρ U D_h / μ; Ri ≈ g(ΔT/T_a)D_h/U². These screen mixing/transition and buoyancy; they do not determine the target temperature by themselves. Exit-speed travel estimate 20–50 cm: 0.15–0.37 s. Real velocity changes along the jet.

3 · Warm-air mixing removes the temperature drop

Tmix = (ṁcTc + ṁaTa) / (ṁc + ṁa)
ΔT = (Ta − Tc) / (1 + E)

E = entrained warm mass / captured cold-source mass. Nozzle engineering aims to limit dilution while covering the target.

For >6°C drop with 30°C ambient and 0.96°C source: E < 3.84, or cold-source fraction >20.7%. At E = 4, drop is only 5.81°C.

Assumptions / derivation

This is an adiabatic dry-air mixing balance with common constant cp. It applies to local captured flows, not automatically to the producer’s entire flow. For a finite mixed parcel, replace mass rates with masses. At fixed E, more flow changes power, not mixture temperature. Actual E changes with geometry, flow, gravity and wind; CFD or measurement must establish it.

4 · Pressure enables expansion and pays flow losses

T2s / T1 = (p2 / p1)(γ−1)/γ
Δploss ≈ (f L/D + ΣK) ρ U²/2

An expander lowers stagnation temperature by extracting work. A regulator is approximately isenthalpic; for ideal air, throttling alone gives no recovered bulk-temperature drop.

7 bar gauge supply − 6.3 bar at running motors leaves only 0.7 bar for treatment, hoses, valves and regulation.

Assumptions / derivation

Use kelvin and absolute pressure in the expansion relation; γ≈1.4. The ideal isentropic value is a limit, not the actual vane-motor exhaust. Actual adiabatic T2≈T1−ηs(T1−T2s). The first law at the loaded point gives Tmotor≈Tin−(Wshaft−Qinto_air)/(ṁcp), neglecting changes in kinetic/potential energy. Four ×840 W at 77.6 g/s and 40°C gives −3.04°C before the assumed path warming. Higher exhaust backpressure reduces expansion ratio. Darcy loss uses local density and low-Mach segment assumptions; large pressure changes require compressible analysis. Pressure, source temperature and flow are coupled in hardware.

5 · Hoses warm small flows more

Tout = Ta + (Tin − Ta) e−UA/(ṁcp)

Longer or poorly insulated cold paths add heat before release. Small mass flow has less heat capacity per second.

Illustration only: 5°C entering air, 30°C surroundings, UA = 1 W/K → 14.8°C at 2 g/s; 5.3°C at 77 g/s.

Assumptions / derivation

UA is overall thermal conductance in W/K, not area alone. The expression assumes steady wall conditions and constant properties; first-pulse cooldown and sunlight are extra effects. This describes a duct heat exchanger. Open-jet warming is mainly ambient mixing, with no fixed duct-wall UA. The real cold-path UA has not been measured.

6 · Rapid warming has no universal time constant

ΔT(t) = ΔT0 e−t/τ
τ = M cp / (ṁexchange cp + UA)

This is a well-mixed, source-off zone model. Smaller zones and stronger warm-air exchange warm faster. Geometry, wind and gravity change that exchange.

A 0.5 s lifetime cannot be assumed from the exponential shape. Colder starting air still increases the available temperature margin.

Assumptions / derivation

M is resident zone mass, not mass flow. Ambient and properties are constant; exchange and UA are assumed fixed. For ΔT0>6 K, time above 6 K is t6=τ ln(ΔT0/6). Hypothetical example: a zone initially at 0.96°C, ambient 30°C and assumed τ=0.5 s crosses 6 K after 0.79 s. This is not a measured or CFD lifetime. A continuous spray replenishes the zone; its energy balance includes the source term. The saved CFD ends at 10 s ON and contains no source-off decay.

Source and steady-zone balance
M cp dT/dt = ṁccp(Tc−T) + ṁexchangecp(Ta−T) + UA(Ta−T) + Q̇load

Well-mixed fixed-mass zone; outflow balances total inflow. Source term stays active while spraying. Positive Qload adds heat. CFD resolves a spatial field instead of assuming this uniform zone.

What a person feels
Q̇skin,conv ≈ h Askin(Tskin − Tlocal air)

Local air temperature, speed, exposed skin area and exposure time control convective heat removal. Humidity affects evaporation; sunlight adds radiant heat. Source cooling power and air-temperature drop alone do not establish comfort. The CFD has no person or skin model.
