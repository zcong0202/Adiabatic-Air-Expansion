No hardware is required to run these next models.

GATE A - VARIABLE DENSITY AND BUOYANCY
Map the selected CAD fluid contour into a 3D domain. Use a thermally coupled,
variable-density low-Mach solver. A horizontal jet with vertical gravity is
not axisymmetric. Repeat the still-air target and conservation checks.
Compare with the current dry passive-scalar approximation before accepting
its margin. Add mesh/domain/turbulence sensitivity for the selected state.

GATE B - WIND AND HOT EXHAUST
Apply 0.5 and 1.0 m/s crosswind in both transverse directions. Include the
commercial source/hot-outlet envelopes only when authoritative CAD is known.
Add its hot branch mass and enthalpy from the paired source point. Keep the
hot exhaust pointing away. Determine target offset, coverage and air mean.
Do not model hot recirculation as a universal vacuum or a fixed 0.5 s decay.

GATE C - PULSES
Export the internal steady nozzle outlet profile to an external transient
domain. Use a specified stock-valve waveform and source response assumption.
Compare startup, 3 s release and repeat duty consistent with the compressor.
Monitor delivered enthalpy and target time series, not animation particles.
The actual source transient map remains an input uncertainty without tests.

GATE D - WALLS, WATER AND HUMAN HEAT TRANSFER
Add PLA/insulation conduction and outside heat pickup. Add moisture transport,
condensation/icing where needed. Introduce a controlled-temperature or heated
person surface. Report incremental skin heat removal relative to an ambient
air stream at matched velocity, then assess the full velocity/cooling benefit.
Choose the target body area and heat-removal objective before comfort claims.

RESOURCE AND STOP POLICY
Each campaign must declare solver physics, parameter bounds, case budget,
quality gates and promotion criteria. Numerically rejected solutions cannot
enter the final comparison. A Lean inequality only certifies the model bounds
actually supplied to it. New results cannot validate the old source map or
physical fit. Manufacturer data and later hardware checks close those gaps.
