AA SYSTEM FORMULATION - 19 OCTOBER 2026
Editable source for the technical note and presentation figures.
No hardware is currently available. All new results are computational.

1. MEETING REQUESTS AND THE ACTUAL DESIGN OBJECTIVE
The demonstration targets a localized air stream. It does not cool 1 m3 of
outdoor air or establish whole-body comfort. No shower build is proposed.
Keep the compressor remote. Use a movable stock pressure hose, a stock cold
source near the target, and a custom low-pressure nozzle. Minimize the length
of the COLD path. Route hot exhaust away from the target. A remote compressor
does not remove vortex exhaust noise. Assess that separately.

Target plane A: 0.05 x 0.05 m, 0.20 m from FINAL nozzle outlet.
J = Ta - (1/A) integral_A T(y,z) dA. Require J > 6 K.
Desired screening margin: J >= 7 K under DECLARED stress assumptions.
Still air first. Crosswind remains a separate three-dimensional problem.
The target is an area mean, not a mass-flux-weighted temperature or skin load.

2. CONSERVATION LAWS
Continuity: d(rho)/dt + div(rho*u) = 0.
Momentum: d(rho*u)/dt + div(rho*u tensor u) = -grad(p)+div(tau)+rho*g.
Total energy: d(rho*E)/dt + div[u*(rho*E+p)]
              = div(tau*u - q)+rho*g dot u.
E = internal energy + |u|^2/2. Fourier heat flux q = -k*grad(T).
A turbulence closure replaces unresolved fluctuations in a RANS calculation.
The current screen simplifies to steady, incompressible, constant-density
RANS with k-omega SST and a passive source scalar theta:
  div(u) = 0.
  div(u*theta) = div[(nu/Pr + nu_t/Pr_t)*grad(theta)].
theta=1 at cold source, theta=0 on incoming ambient boundaries.
Pr=0.705, Pr_t=0.85. These are modeling choices, not fitted AA data.
An axisymmetric wedge represents a round nozzle in still air. It cannot
represent horizontal-jet buoyancy, a body, crosswind, or an offset hot plume.

3. CONTROL-VOLUME ENERGY AND SOURCE METHODS
Steady device: mdot*(h_out-h_in + (Vout^2-Vin^2)/2 + g*dz)=Qdot-Wdot.
Low-speed bulk source states below neglect net kinetic and potential changes.

Regulator: Qdot=0, Wdot=0 => h_out=h_in (isenthalpic).
Ideal gas h=cp*T => T_out=T_in. Real gas T_out=T(Pout,h_in).
Use absolute pressures and a real-gas EOS for high-pressure throttling.
Ideal expansion with shaft work: s_out=s_in (isentropic limit).
T_is/T_in=(Pout/Pin)^[(gamma-1)/gamma], temperatures in KELVIN.
Real expander: eta_is=(h_in-h_out)/(h_in-h_is).
Actual work extraction and hardware maps determine the attainable state.
A limit of 3:1 per stage is an assumed design constraint, not a universal law.
Under that assumption 200/5=40 requires at least 4 stages, since 3^3=27.
No matched commercial expander is identified. Turbine chains are excluded
from the stock-component build shortlist. Their thermodynamic bounds remain
available in the earlier calculator for explanation only.

Vortex tube: mdot_c=beta*mdot_in; mdot_h=(1-beta)*mdot_in.
Adiabatic no-work dry balance: T_in=beta*T_c+(1-beta)*T_h.
T_h=(T_in-beta*T_c)/(1-beta).
The balance alone does not predict T_c or beta. Use a paired supplier map.
Current study: approximate EXAIR family curve at 100 psig, candidate 3208,
227 SLPM inlet. beta=0.6/0.7/0.8 gives nominal drops 86/71/54 F
(47.78/39.44/30 K). With inlet 34 C, T_c=-13.78/-5.44/+4 C.
The family curve's applicability to the selected generator and full assembly
must be confirmed. It is not Jeanoko performance data.
SLPM-to-mass conversion here ASSUMES 20 C and 101325 Pa. A 10% low-flow
case is a declared stress. It does not establish the actual reference basis.
Do not use the catalogue's separate nominal cooling-capacity rating to force
agreement with the approximate curve. Confirm matched rating conditions.

4. NOZZLE AND DELIVERY
At assumed rho: V_exit=mdot_c/(rho*A_exit).
Re=rho*V_exit*D/mu. Ma=V/c_sound.
Ri ~ g*(Ta-Tdelivery)/(Ta+273.15)*L/V_target^2.
Ri is a warning about buoyancy significance, not a correction to this solver.
For an adiabatic passive nozzle h+V^2/2 is conserved. Lower static temperature
from acceleration does not remove stagnation enthalpy. A temperature sensor
in fast flow may not read the relevant recovered bulk temperature.

Custom axisymmetric cone: inlet bore 8 mm, outlet bore D, half angle a.
Cone length=(D-8)/(2*tan(a)); total length=cone length+15+10 mm.
Wall radial thickness=2 mm. The 8 mm source interface is an abstraction.
Manufacturer CAD/thread/bore data are still needed for an EXAIR fitted adapter.
The historical Milton/Jeanoko fit does not prove an EXAIR mounting fit.
The CFD matches the internal contour. It omits the exterior nozzle body and
uses a zero-thickness outlet lip. The printed 2 mm wall/lip needs full 3D
geometry verification. The lumped U uses the inner wetted reference area.

Cold-path heat pickup: mdot_c*cp*dT/dx = U*Pwall*(Tsurround-T).
For constant surroundings and integrated UA:
r=exp[-UA/(mdot_c*cp)]; Td=Te-(Te-Ts)*r.
UA is W/K. U is W/(m2 K). Do not confuse them.
Cone wall area = pi*(r_in+r_out)*sqrt(Lcone^2+(r_out-r_in)^2)
                + pi*D_in*L_in + pi*D_out*L_out.
The current screen uses UA=U*wall_area and Te for both entrainment and wall
surroundings. This is a one-way lumped correction, not conjugate CFD.
If Ts already means the measured final outlet, exclude upstream heat pickup.

5. AREA-AVERAGE MIXING AND STRICT FEASIBILITY
Dry equal-cp local law: T(y,z)=Te-theta(y,z)*(Te-Td).
alpha_A=(1/A) integral_A theta dA.
J=Ta-Te+alpha_A*r*(Te-Ts).
For nonuniform velocity alpha_A differs from cold mass CAPTURE efficiency,
the mass-flux-weighted source fraction, and the vortex split beta.

Let C=delta+Te-Ta and B=alpha_A*(Te-Ts). With positive denominators:
  J>delta iff alpha_A > C/[r*(Te-Ts)].
  J>delta iff Ts < Te-C/(alpha_A*r).
  J>delta iff UA < mdot_c*cp*ln(B/C).
  For B>C>0, mdot_c > UA/[cp*ln(B/C)] at FIXED alpha_A.
The last expression is a heat-retention bound, not an overall flow design.
If B<=C, mass alone cannot rescue the target at fixed source and dilution.
The geometry-to-alpha relation must come from CFD or data.
For the selected 28 mm reference geometry at 90% flow, the saved dry CFD
gives about 1.485 g/s captured cold-origin air and 3.109 g/s entrained-origin
air through the target. Source delivery is 2.460 g/s; not all of it crosses
the 5 x 5 cm target. The calculated source capture is about 60.4%.
Area source fraction is about 31.62%; mass-flux-weighted fraction is 32.32%.
These are CFD outputs under the declared model, not measured hardware data.

Alternative UNIFORM target: captured cold m=eta_capture*mdot_c; warm inflow mw.
Tmix=[m*Td+mw*Te+Q_extra/cp]/(m+mw).
Pass iff m*(Ta-Td-delta) > mw*(Te-Ta+delta)+Q_extra/cp.
Scaling both incoming mass flows equally leaves the loss-free Tmix unchanged.
It raises power, but it does not automatically improve temperature.

6. MOISTURE, STRESSES AND EVIDENCE
For moist mixing, conserve dry-air mass, water mass and enthalpy.
With humidity ratios w_i and local transported humid-air fraction f:
f_dry=[f/(1+w_c)]/[f/(1+w_c)+(1-f)/(1+w_a)].
w_mix=f_dry*w_c+(1-f_dry)*w_a.
h_mix=f_dry*h_c+(1-f_dry)*h_a.
If the resulting state supersaturates, solve for equilibrium temperature,
including retained liquid-water enthalpy. At target temperatures above 0 C
the current numerical companion uses liquid water, not ice.
Source moisture uses an ASSUMED +3 C pressure dew point at the vortex inlet.
That is a dryer selection requirement, not a verified quoted-dryer rating.
The bulk outlet check does not exclude colder internal vortex regions or
surface icing. The supplier's required dew point may demand a stock
desiccant dryer. A +3 C pressure dew point is a numerical assumption here,
not confirmation that a refrigerated dryer is sufficient for the assembly.
This does not simulate droplets, ice, surface wetting, or water separation.

Declared stresses combine: 20% smaller source temperature drop, source inlet
4 K above ambient, entrained/wall surroundings 2 K above ambient, 10% lower
source mass (new CFD), additional 10% erosion of calculated area fraction,
U=20 W/(m2 K), ambient RH=80%, and pressure dew point +3 C.
These ranges are proposed engineering screens. They are not manufacturer
bounds, statistical intervals, or experimentally calibrated uncertainty.
Recompute theta for source flow or geometry changes. Frozen-theta screening
cannot establish the response of a new operating point.

7. COMPRESSOR, HOSE AND BUFFER
FAD mass: mdot=P_ref*Q_ref/(R*T_ref). Do not multiply reference L/min by bar.
Mean supply constraint: duty*N*Q_in <= Q_compressor on a common reference.
BAUER 100 vs one 227 L/min source: ideal duty <=0.4405.
A 3 s pulse needs period >=6.81 s under this ideal average mass balance.
Kaneez quoted 1000-1200 L/min could support 4 such sources by ideal inlet
flow alone. Pressure-specific FAD, rated duty and dryer capacity are unverified.
One vortex does not consume the whole compressor output automatically.

Peak inventory swing >=max(0,mdot_demand-mdot_production)*pulse_duration.
Uniform isothermal ideal-gas receiver inventory swing:
Delta M=V*(P_high-P_low)/(R*T), with absolute pressures.
Fast blowdown cools the receiver, changes pressure and delivery, and requires
a transient gas/wall energy model. This formula is not a pressure-vessel design.
A stock compressed-air receiver stores feed mass. It is not a cold-air tank.
Cold buffer energy deficit E=M*cp*(Ta-Tbuffer) needs its own charging,
withdrawal, heat leakage and pressure balance. A buffer cannot improve the
average source energy budget or prevent dilution after discharge.

Use a ~10 m COMPRESSED feed hose and place the vortex near the custom nozzle.
For a smooth turbulent hose: dp=f_D*(L/D)*rho*V^2/2, f_D~0.3164/Re^0.25.
The companion computes a mean-density screen. Add filters, dryer, fittings
and regulator headroom. Stock component Cv/flow curves must close the chain.
An ideal isothermal compression lower bound is Wdot_min=mdot*R*T*ln(P2/P1).
Actual electrical demand includes compressor inefficiency, cooling, dryer,
idle cycling and controls. The motor-nameplate conversion is not actual COP.
Report cold sensible potential Qcold=mdot_c*cp*(Ta-Td), target advected
deficit integral_A rho*cp*u_normal*(Ta-T)dA, and electrical power separately.
None of these alone equals heat removed from a person.
For a person-surface model: Qskin=integral_A h(y,z)*(Tskin-Tair) dA.
At fixed, uniform h and skin temperature, incremental heat removal is
Delta Qskin=h*A*Delta Tair. For an ILLUSTRATIVE assumed h=20 W/(m2 K),
A=0.0025 m2 and Delta Tair=6 K, this is 0.30 W. The chosen h is not an AA
measurement or a comfort prediction. Higher airspeed changes h as well as
entrainment. A source enthalpy flow of ~130 W is not ~130 W delivered to skin.

8. WHAT LEAN PROVES
The pinned project proves dry mixing/area identities, exact strict bounds,
heat-retention and exposure-time inequalities, source-method implications,
vortex branch energy balance, supply/duty/inventory bounds, a conservative
area-envelope theorem, and the nozzle static/kinetic energy identity.
Given alpha>=amin, r>=rmin, Te-Ts>=spanmin, Ta-Te>=-hot with nonnegative
lower bounds, it proves J >= -hot+amin*rmin*spanmin.
The proof does not establish that physical source/CFD errors obey those bounds.
Concrete stock-reference certificate: alpha_A >=0.27, mc>=0.0024 kg/s,
UA<=0.16 W/K, Te-Ts>=36 K, Ta-Te>=-2 K and added moisture warming<=0.5 K
give J>=-2+0.27*0.93*36-0.5=6.5396 K>6 K.
Lean proves retention>=0.93 from those mass/UA bounds using exp(x)>=1+x.
No floating exponential is trusted for that implication. Numerical screening
checks whether a computed case supplies these inputs. That check does not
prove the empirical source, CFD closure or moisture model physically correct.
The Lean kernel checks symbolic consequences. Floating EOS, psychrometrics,
PDE discretization, RANS closure and the hardware remain outside that proof.

9. RECURSIVE COMPUTATIONAL WORKFLOW AND STOP RULE
Read stock maps and freeze source/installation constraints.
Generate candidate CAD parameters and source operating point together.
Reject flow/pressure/duty mismatch and impossible thermal budgets.
Run a fresh CFD solution for each changed flow or geometry.
Check mesh, solver end, residuals, native balances, bounded scalar,
target stability and full-field stability. Failed cases cannot enter ranking.
Apply declared heat, source, entrainment and moisture stresses.
Use the result to choose the next paired source state or nozzle dimension.
Recompute the low-flow corner. Refine a passing mesh and compare the result.
Stop when the declared model envelope meets the margin and mesh gate, or
when the six-case campaign budget ends. Preserve failures and next questions.
The result is a bounded computational shortlist, not a global hardware optimum.
Next physical models: 3D variable-density/buoyant flow, crosswind, hot exhaust,
transients, wall heating, moisture transport, and person heat transfer.
No hardware is required to run those models. Hardware is eventually required
to establish source maps, mounting fit, noise and actual delivered performance.
