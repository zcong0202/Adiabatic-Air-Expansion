Scuba → motor → vortex tube → cold outlet

User requested series operation. One AM0701 drives the friction brake. Its pressurized exhaust feeds the vortex inlet. The vortex then separates that air into a cold stream and a hot stream. This is physically possible in principle; no qualified operating point has been established for the actual AM0701 and Jeanoko pair.

The pressure constraint

P1 > P2 > ambient. Both devices share one available pressure drop. The earlier motor calculation assumed exhaust close to ambient. It cannot be carried over when the vortex forces the motor exhaust to stay pressurized.

EXAIR’s FAQ describes normal vortex inlet pressures of 5.5–6.9 bar gauge and low allowed cold-outlet backpressure. These are EXAIR references, not verified Jeanoko specifications. The AM0701 page publishes a 1–7 bar operating range, with pressure basis and permitted exhaust backpressure still requiring confirmation.

Illustrative motor inlet: 6 bar gauge = 7 bar absolute, 22°C. The ideal-gas, reversible adiabatic reference is ΔTideal = Tin,K [1 − (Pout,abs / Pin,abs)^((γ−1)/γ)], with γ=1.4. Keeping 5.5 bar gauge at the vortex inlet leaves only 0.5 bar pressure difference across the motor before line loss.

Cold mass also changes

ṁvortex,in = ṁmotor
ṁcold = α ṁmotor
ṁhot = (1−α) ṁmotor

α is the vortex cold fraction. The final cold outlet receives only a fraction of the motor exhaust. Its actual value and the coupled flow are unknown. The previous 15 g/s motor screen is not a prediction of final cold-nozzle mass flow. A restrictive vortex may reduce motor flow further.

Reverse order

Vortex → motor is also pressure-coupled: driving the motor requires retaining pressure at the vortex cold outlet. EXAIR warns that cold-outlet backpressure reduces vortex cooling; its normal outlet condition does not establish a useful downstream motor operating point. The Jeanoko limit is unknown. Reversing the order is not a qualified solution.

What must be established

Obtain the AM0701 permitted exhaust backpressure and loaded torque/RPM/flow map at that backpressure. Obtain the exact vortex inlet pressure/flow and cold-fraction map at the resulting inlet temperature. Verify pressure-rated exhaust collection, seals, lubrication/oil carryover and low-temperature limits. Then evaluate both components at a common flow. Temperature drops from separate tests cannot simply be added.
