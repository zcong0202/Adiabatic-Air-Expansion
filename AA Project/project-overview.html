Hard limits

  • Finite capacity: Mass flow and recovered cold temperature set the available sensible cooling. Colder minimum temperature can coincide with less cold flow. Optimize useful heat removal.
  • Wind and distance: Low-speed cold flow bends in crosswind and sinks. A compact local outlet has a better prospect at 20 cm than a large open-air cooled volume.
  • Pressure and heat gain: Added delivery resistance can change vortex output. Long cold ducts warm from the surroundings. Exhaust recirculation can erase useful cooling.
  • Humidity: Cold surfaces can condense ambient moisture. Condensation changes the sensible-only balance and can wet probes. Dry supply limits internal icing but does not prevent ambient condensation.
  • Comfort and resource use: Draft, noise, target coverage and radiation affect the result. Include compressor electricity, compressed-air use and the rejected hot stream in the comparison. Stored air also has a charging cost.
